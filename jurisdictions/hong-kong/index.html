Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in Hong Kong concerns how controlled transactions between associated persons are priced and evidenced for Hong Kong profits tax purposes under the arm's length principle. The legal framework is contained in Part 8AA of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), including section 50AAF for transactions between associated persons and section 50AAK for attributing profits to a permanent establishment.

In operational terms, Hong Kong applies the OECD BEPS Action 13 three-tier documentation approach of master file, local file and country-by-country report. Master file and local file requirements apply for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2018. An entity may be exempt from both files if it satisfies any two of the business-size thresholds, or may be exempt from local-file coverage for a particular transaction category where that category remains below the applicable statutory threshold.

The business-size exemption applies where an entity satisfies any two of these conditions: total revenue does not exceed HK$400 million; total assets do not exceed HK$300 million; and the average number of employees does not exceed 100. For local-file category exemptions, the thresholds are HK$220 million for transfers of properties excluding financial assets and intangibles, HK$110 million for financial assets, HK$110 million for intangibles, and HK$44 million for other transactions. The files must be prepared within 9 months after the end of the Hong Kong entity's accounting period and retained for at least 7 years.

Hong Kong also applies CbCR to qualifying groups with consolidated revenue of at least HK$6.8 billion or EUR 750 million, and offers unilateral, bilateral and multilateral APAs. An APA usually covers 3 to 5 years and requires annual reporting. This makes Hong Kong transfer pricing particularly important for regional headquarters, trading, logistics, finance, treasury, services, IP, investment, China-facing and wider Asia-Pacific group structures.

Purpose

The purpose of the transfer pricing function is to ensure that controlled transactions connected to Hong Kong are priced consistently with the arm's length principle and can be explained through credible facts, analysis, Action 13 documentation and prescribed tax reporting.

It exists to reduce profits tax adjustment risk, support master-file and local-file compliance, improve Inland Revenue Department audit readiness and create a coherent evidentiary position for APA, MAP and cross-border profit-allocation management.

A defensible Hong Kong transfer pricing position in which the relevant controlled transactions, method, comparable support, master file, local file, CbCR, Supplementary Form S2 position and cross-border coordination are aligned with the arm's length principle and the actual conduct of the parties.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Hong Kong transfer pricing work usually progresses from controlled transaction mapping to documentation, tax return disclosure and possible APA or controversy management. It matters because Hong Kong uses both business-size and transaction-category thresholds to determine the applicable master-file and local-file scope.

1. Controlled Transaction MappingIdentify the controlled transactions connected to Hong Kong, including goods, services, financing, licensing, IP, trading, logistics, regional management and permanent establishment dealings.
2. Business-Size Exemption AssessmentAssess revenue, assets and average employee count to determine whether the Hong Kong entity satisfies any two conditions for exemption from both master file and local file.
3. Transaction-Category AssessmentAssess each controlled transaction category against the statutory thresholds to determine whether local-file coverage is required for that category.
4. Functional Analysis and Method SelectionAnalyse actual functions, assets, risks, contractual terms and value creation, then choose the most appropriate OECD-consistent method.
5. Master File and Local File BuildPrepare the files within 9 months after accounting-period end, including functional, comparable, financial and agreement support.
6. Profits Tax Return and S2 DisclosureAlign the Hong Kong profits tax return and Supplementary Form S2 with the controlled transaction data and documentation position.
7. IRD Audit, APA or MAP RouteIf uncertainty or controversy arises, respond to an Inland Revenue Department request or pursue unilateral, bilateral or multilateral APA or treaty MAP as relevant.
Typical OutputsMaster file, local file, functional analysis, benchmarking, intercompany agreements, Supplementary Form S2 support, CbCR support, audit response papers and APA documentation.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine the right Hong Kong transfer pricing approach.

  1. Identify whether the arrangement is a controlled transaction between associated persons or has Hong Kong permanent establishment profit attribution relevance.
  2. Confirm which party performs the key functions, controls economically significant risks and contributes relevant assets or intangibles.
  3. Assess whether any two business-size exemption criteria are satisfied: revenue not above HK$400 million, assets not above HK$300 million and average employees not above 100.
  4. If no business-size exemption applies, test each transaction category against HK$220 million for properties, HK$110 million for financial assets, HK$110 million for intangibles and HK$44 million for other transactions.
  5. Choose the most appropriate OECD-consistent method and prepare the master file and relevant local-file transaction coverage within 9 months after period end.
  6. Assess CbCR status, align Supplementary Form S2 and decide whether unilateral, bilateral or multilateral APA or MAP planning is appropriate.

Operating Constraints & Risks

Operating constraints identify the recurring friction points that affect transfer pricing execution in Hong Kong.

Dual Exemption RiskConfusing the business-size exemption with the transaction-category exemption can lead to an incorrect conclusion that no master file or local file is required.
Category Classification RiskTransactions must be correctly classified as property, financial asset, intangible or other transaction to apply the correct local-file threshold.
9-Month Deadline RiskMaster file and local file must be prepared within 9 months after the entity's own accounting-period end, irrespective of any later profits tax return filing date.
Documentation Retention RiskMaster file and local file must be retained for at least 7 years after the end of the accounting period, requiring reliable document management.
Penalty ExposureFailure to prepare or retain required transfer pricing documentation can attract statutory penalties even if the taxpayer considers the controlled transaction pricing to be arm's length.
