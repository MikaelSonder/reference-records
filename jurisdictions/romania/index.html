Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in Romania concerns how related-party transactions are priced and evidenced for tax purposes under the arm's length principle. Romanian law refers to that principle through Article 11 of Law no. 227/2015 on the Fiscal Code, which expressly incorporates the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines by reference, giving them binding legal effect alongside domestic statute.

In operational terms, the Romanian framework combines a codified arm's length rule with a detailed administrative regime for documentation, method selection and comparability analysis. ANAF Order no. 442/2016 originally set the content, thresholds and adjustment procedure for the transfer pricing file, and this framework was substantially overhauled by ANAF Order no. 828/2026.

Documentation is a central and increasingly formalised part of the system. Romania distinguishes between large taxpayers exceeding materiality thresholds, who must prepare the file annually and submit it electronically through the Private Virtual Space (SPV) within 30 working days of the corporate income tax filing deadline, and smaller taxpayers who must prepare the file only upon request during a tax audit.

Romania also offers dispute-prevention and dispute-resolution tools through unilateral, bilateral and multilateral advance pricing agreements, with new APA procedural rules introduced under ANAF Order no. 827/2026, including the possibility of retroactive coverage for prior closed fiscal years. This makes Romanian transfer pricing especially relevant for multinational groups using Romania for manufacturing, shared services, distribution or regional operating structures.

Purpose

The purpose of the transfer pricing function is to ensure that controlled transactions connected to Romania are priced consistently with the arm's length principle and can be explained through credible facts, comparability analysis and documentation.

It exists to reduce tax risk, improve cross-border defensibility, support consistent group pricing and create a workable evidentiary position if the National Agency for Fiscal Administration (ANAF) reviews the transaction.

Primary Outcome

A defensible Romanian transfer pricing position in which the relevant controlled transactions, pricing method, comparability support, documentation file and cross-border coordination are aligned with the arm's length principle and the operational reality of the group.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Romanian transfer pricing work usually progresses from transaction mapping to documentation and possible controversy management. It matters because transfer pricing is not a single memo, but a sequence of factual, analytical and procedural steps.

1. Transaction MappingIdentify the controlled transactions connected to Romania and determine which entities, functions, assets and risks are involved.
2. Threshold AssessmentAssess which materiality threshold category applies and whether annual or on-request documentation is triggered.
3. Functional AnalysisAnalyse what each party actually does, controls and assumes in operational and economic terms.
4. Method Selection and Comparability StudyChoose the most appropriate method and prepare a comparability study following the geographic hierarchy set by ANAF Order no. 828/2026.
5. Documentation BuildPrepare the transfer pricing file, including the comparability study in the required Excel format with search strategy and selection criteria.
6. Return and Electronic SubmissionAlign the Romanian tax return position and, for large taxpayers, submit the file electronically via SPV within 30 working days of the applicable deadline.
7. Audit or APA RouteIf uncertainty or controversy arises, move into audit response or unilateral, bilateral or multilateral APA application as relevant.
Typical OutputsFunctional analysis, comparability study, pricing policy support, transfer pricing file, intercompany alignment notes, audit response papers and APA documentation.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine the right Romanian transfer pricing approach.

  1. Identify whether the transaction is between related parties and has Romanian tax relevance.
  2. Confirm which party performs the key functions, bears the economically significant risks and controls the relevant assets.
  3. Determine whether the taxpayer is a large taxpayer exceeding the annual materiality thresholds or a smaller taxpayer subject to on-request documentation only.
  4. Determine whether the pricing can be tested more reliably with a traditional transaction method or a profit-based method under OECD practice as applied in Romania.
  5. Assess whether a comparability study is required and which geographic hierarchy of comparables applies.
  6. Decide whether ordinary documentation is sufficient or whether prospective or retroactive certainty through an APA should be considered.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Romania Apply the Arm's Length Principle to Related-Party Transactions?Yes. Romania applies the arm's length principle through Article 11 of Law no. 227/2015 on the Fiscal Code.
Does Romania Follow the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines in Practice?Yes. Romanian law directly incorporates the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines by reference, giving them binding interpretative effect.
Does Romania Require a Transfer Pricing File?Yes. Romania requires a transfer pricing file under ANAF Order no. 442/2016, substantially revised by ANAF Order no. 828/2026, with obligations depending on materiality thresholds.
Must Large Taxpayers Submit the File Electronically?Yes. Large taxpayers must submit the file via the Private Virtual Space (SPV) within 30 working days of the corporate income tax filing deadline.
