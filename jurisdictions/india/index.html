Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in India concerns the pricing of international transactions between associated enterprises and specified domestic transactions. The Indian framework applies the arm's length principle through detailed statutory and rule-based provisions, supported by an extensive documentation regime and a specialised tax-administration process involving transfer pricing officers.

India's transfer pricing environment is especially significant for multinational groups operating technology, IT-enabled services, contract research and development, manufacturing, distribution, financing and intangible-related arrangements. The system requires a rigorous factual record: Rule 10D specifies ownership information, group profile, transaction terms, functional analysis, economic analysis, uncontrolled comparables, method selection, arm's length workings, assumptions and adjustment records.

From 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 2025 and final Income-tax Rules, 2026 introduced an updated compliance framework. The reforms rationalised safe harbour rules, modernised APA procedures and introduced Form 48 to replace the former Form 3CEB for arm's length reporting. The detailed recordkeeping architecture remains central, and documentation should be contemporaneous and retained for eight years from the end of the relevant assessment year.

India also operates one of the world's largest APA programmes. In FY 2025-26, the Central Board of Direct Taxes signed 219 APAs, taking the cumulative total since the programme began to 1,034. The programme includes unilateral and bilateral APAs, while safe harbour rules provide a separate, standardised route for qualifying transaction categories. This makes Indian transfer pricing a core strategic tax function rather than a purely annual compliance exercise.

Purpose

The purpose of the transfer pricing function is to ensure that international and specified domestic transactions connected to India are priced consistently with the arm's length principle and can be supported through credible facts, analysis, records and statutory reporting.

It exists to reduce adjustment risk, improve audit readiness, support consistent group pricing and create a workable evidentiary position if the Indian tax administration or a Transfer Pricing Officer reviews the transaction.

Primary Outcome

A defensible Indian transfer pricing position in which the relevant controlled transactions, pricing method, comparable support, documentation, Form 48 reporting, safe harbour or APA position and cross-border coordination are aligned with the arm's length principle and the group's actual conduct.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics matter because India combines a very large cross-border services economy with a detailed transfer pricing statute, specialised audit process and established certainty mechanisms. Indian practice is particularly shaped by the prevalence of captive IT, IT-enabled services, R&D and support-function arrangements, as well as by the practical importance of comparable selection and margin analysis.

Operational CultureIndia is highly documentation-focused and assessment-sensitive, with detailed review of functional profiles, comparable companies, margins and segmental financial data.
Legal Framework OrientationThe Indian framework is statute- and rule-based, supported by detailed documentation requirements, prescribed reporting and administrative transfer pricing review.
Commercial ContextIndia is a major hub for software development, IT-enabled services, business process outsourcing, knowledge-process outsourcing, contract R&D, manufacturing and regional group services.
Certainty FrameworkIndia combines litigation and assessment procedures with safe harbour rules and one of the world's most active APA programmes.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Indian transfer pricing work usually progresses from transaction mapping to documentation, statutory reporting and possible controversy management. It matters because Indian transfer pricing depends on detailed contemporaneous facts, economic support and procedural readiness.

1. Transaction MappingIdentify international and specified domestic transactions connected to India and determine the relevant associated enterprises, value chains, functions, assets and risks.
2. Functional AnalysisAnalyse what each party actually does, controls and assumes, including the Indian entity's role as service provider, manufacturer, distributor, R&D centre or entrepreneur.
3. Method SelectionChoose the most appropriate method and identify the tested party, profit level indicator or transaction-level metric suitable for the facts.
4. Comparability ReviewReview internal or external comparables, apply appropriate filters and adjustments, and test the outcome against the arm's length range.
5. Documentation BuildPrepare contemporaneous records covering ownership, group, transactions, functional analysis, economic analysis, comparable data, method selection and pricing workings.
6. Form 48 and ComplianceObtain the prescribed accountant's report and align the income tax return, reportable transactions and supporting evidence.
7. Safe Harbour, Audit or APA RouteWhere appropriate, elect safe harbour, prepare for a TPO review or pursue unilateral or bilateral APA certainty.
Typical OutputsTransfer pricing study, functional analysis, benchmarking, Form 48 support, segmented financials, safe harbour election support, audit response papers and APA documentation.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold and approach questions that commonly determine the right Indian transfer pricing strategy.

  1. Identify whether the arrangement is an international transaction or a specified domestic transaction with Indian tax relevance.
  2. Confirm the associated enterprises, transaction classes, functions, assets, risks and the economic character of the Indian entity.
  3. Determine whether full contemporaneous documentation is required, including whether the aggregate international transaction value exceeds the applicable documentation threshold.
  4. Select the most appropriate method and prepare comparability analysis, keeping in mind the Indian assessment environment and available financial data.
  5. Assess whether the transaction falls within an available safe harbour category and whether a safe harbour election is commercially preferable.
  6. Decide whether normal compliance is sufficient or whether prospective certainty through unilateral or bilateral APA should be considered.

Cross-border relevance is central because Indian transfer pricing is designed primarily around international transactions and the allocation of taxable profit between India and foreign associated enterprises. The Indian position must be consistent with group policy, real conduct, treaty context and the documentation position of counterparties in other jurisdictions.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does India Apply the Arm's Length Principle to International Transactions?Yes. India applies the arm's length principle to international transactions and specified domestic transactions through its transfer pricing legislation and rules.
Does India Require Detailed Transfer Pricing Documentation?Yes. The documentation regime requires detailed ownership, transaction, functional, economic, comparable, method and pricing-workings records.
What Is the Rule 10D Documentation Threshold for International Transactions?The full Rule 10D-type documentation requirement does not apply where the aggregate recorded value of international transactions does not exceed Rs. 1 crore, although the taxpayer must still substantiate that income has been computed in accordance with the arm's length principle.
How Long Must Indian Transfer Pricing Records Be Retained?Documentation is to be kept and maintained for eight years from the end of the relevant assessment year.
Does India Offer Safe Harbour Rules?Yes. India offers safe harbour for specified categories of international transactions; Finance Act 2026 introduced significant enhancements, including a unified 15.5% margin for Information Technology Services and a Rs. 2000 crore eligibility threshold.
Does India Offer Unilateral and Bilateral APAs?Yes. CBDT operates unilateral and bilateral APA programmes. APAs can provide advance certainty for up to five years, while bilateral APAs can help mitigate double-taxation risk.
