Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in Georgia concerns the pricing and Georgia corporate income tax treatment of controlled and related-party transactions. The substantive arm's length benchmark is principally drawn from United States federal Internal Revenue Code section 482 and Treasury Regulations section 1.482. Georgia corporate income tax begins with federal taxable income, making federal transfer pricing adjustments, functional analysis, method selection and contemporaneous documentation directly relevant to the Georgia state tax base.

In operational terms, Georgia differs from mandatory unitary combined-reporting states. Georgia does not generally require unitary combined reporting. Instead, Georgia provides an affiliated group election framework for corporations eligible to be members of a federal affiliated group. A Georgia affiliated group return may use the federal consolidated return as its starting point, subject to Georgia-specific rules on which corporations are included and how Georgia taxable income is calculated.

A key Georgia-specific feature is separate member apportionment. For allocation and apportionment, each member of a Georgia affiliated group is considered a separate taxpayer. Each member calculates its own Georgia apportionment and separately determines the portion of its income attributable to Georgia. Accordingly, the economic and legal treatment of intercompany transactions, member-level financial results, nexus, sales sourcing and apportionment remains important even when an affiliated group files a consolidated state return.

Georgia has no separate state transfer pricing local file, master file or APA programme. The core defensibility package is therefore the federal IRC Section 482 study together with Georgia affiliated group election records, legal entity financials, intercompany agreements, federal-to-Georgia return reconciliations and member-specific allocation and apportionment workpapers. This is especially relevant for logistics, manufacturing, automotive, film and media, technology, data centres, financial services, healthcare, energy, private equity and multinational groups operating in Georgia or through the Atlanta regional business hub.

Purpose

The purpose of the Georgia transfer pricing function is to ensure that controlled and related-party transactions are arm's length under IRC Section 482 where relevant, correctly reflected in Georgia corporate taxable income and accurately allocated and apportioned by each member of a Georgia affiliated group.

It exists to reduce federal and Georgia adjustment risk, support a valid affiliated group filing position, preserve defensible intercompany deductions and income recognition, reconcile member-level financial results and maintain documentation suitable for Georgia Department of Revenue review.

Primary Outcome

A defensible Georgia transfer pricing and corporate income tax position in which controlled transactions, IRC Section 482 method support, affiliated group composition, legal entity financial results, intercompany income and deductions, separate member allocation and apportionment are aligned with Georgia law and the actual operating model.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Georgia transfer pricing work usually progresses from legal entity and transaction mapping to federal documentation, affiliated group analysis and potential Department of Revenue review. It matters because Georgia combines federal transfer pricing dependence with a state affiliated group system in which each member is separately apportioned.

1. Entity, Ownership and Nexus MappingIdentify legal entities, Georgia nexus, ownership relationships, federal affiliated group members, foreign affiliates and the group structure relevant to Georgia corporate income tax.
2. Georgia Affiliated Group AssessmentDetermine whether the corporations are eligible and elect to file a Georgia affiliated group return, identify included and excluded entities and document the federal consolidated return relationship.
3. Intercompany Transaction MappingIdentify goods, services, royalties, financing, IP, management fee and other controlled transactions, distinguishing dealings among affiliated group members from transactions with related parties outside the Georgia group.
4. IRC Section 482 Functional and Method AnalysisConduct functional analysis, select the best method, test comparables and maintain federal arm's length documentation for controlled transactions relevant to federal and Georgia review.
5. Georgia Separate Member Income ReviewDetermine how intercompany income and deductions, federal return items, Georgia modifications and related-party transactions affect each member's separate Georgia income position.
6. Separate Apportionment and Return AlignmentCalculate each member's Georgia allocation and apportionment, prepare affiliated group return schedules, sales factor workpapers and reconciliations to federal and entity financial data.
7. Georgia DOR Audit, Federal APA or Controversy RouteIf reviewed, provide ownership, group election, entity financials, agreements, functional analysis, Section 482 documentation and member-specific apportionment workpapers to Georgia DOR.
Typical OutputsFederal IRC Section 482 study, functional analysis, benchmarking, Georgia affiliated group map, group election support, intercompany transaction matrix, separate member financial workpapers, apportionment schedules, legal agreements and audit response package.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies the questions that commonly determine the correct Georgia transfer pricing and corporate income tax approach.

  1. Identify whether the transaction is between controlled or related parties and has federal or Georgia corporate income tax relevance.
  2. Determine the Georgia nexus and federal affiliated group status of each relevant legal entity.
  3. Assess whether a Georgia affiliated group consolidated return is elected or required under the applicable state rules and identify the included members.
  4. Recognise that Georgia member allocation and apportionment are generally computed separately even within an affiliated group return.
  5. For all controlled transactions, maintain robust IRC Section 482 functional analysis, method selection, comparable support, financial evidence and intercompany agreements.
  6. Reconcile federal taxable income, Georgia modifications, group return data, member-level income, sales factor and apportionment before filing and throughout Georgia Department of Revenue examination readiness.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Georgia Apply IRC Section 482 Principles to Related-Party Transfer Pricing?Yes. Georgia corporate income tax begins with federal taxable income, so federal IRC Section 482 adjustments and documentation are directly relevant. Georgia may also require allocation, apportionment or separate accounting where necessary to fairly represent Georgia taxable income.
Does Georgia Require Unitary Combined Reporting?No. Georgia does not impose mandatory unitary combined reporting. Georgia affiliated group reporting follows a state election framework based on members of the federal affiliated group, and each member is separately apportioned and treated as a separate taxpayer for allocation and apportionment.
What Is a Georgia Affiliated Group Return?A Georgia affiliated group may elect to file a consolidated corporate income tax return. The group generally consists of corporations eligible to be members of the federal affiliated group, subject to Georgia-specific inclusion and nexus rules. Each member remains separately apportioned for Georgia purposes.
Are Intercompany Transactions Eliminated in a Georgia Affiliated Group Return?Not in the same full unitary-combined-report manner used by states such as California or New York. Georgia affiliated group reporting generally follows the federal consolidated return starting point, while each member is separately apportioned for Georgia purposes. Intercompany items must be reconciled carefully with Georgia-specific member and apportionment rules.
Does Georgia Require a Separate State Transfer Pricing Local File?No. Georgia has no standalone state local-file or master-file form. Taxpayers should retain federal IRC Section 482 documentation, Georgia affiliated group election support, separate member financials, intercompany agreements and Georgia apportionment workpapers for Department of Revenue review.
Why Is Separate Member Apportionment Important in Georgia?Each member of a Georgia affiliated group is treated as a separate taxpayer for allocation and apportionment. A group return therefore does not eliminate the need to identify each member's income, sales factors, nexus and Georgia share of group income.
