Cross-Border Note
Wyoming imposes no state corporate income tax, no general state franchise tax and no state transfer pricing regime. Transfer pricing for a Wyoming entity remains primarily governed by federal IRC Section 482 and the rules of every state and foreign jurisdiction where the entity actually performs functions, owns assets, controls risks or does business.
Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in Wyoming has a distinctive jurisdictional profile: Wyoming does not impose a state corporate income tax and does not maintain a separate state transfer pricing regime, state combined reporting system, state local file or state APA programme. A Wyoming corporation, LLC, partnership or holding company may nevertheless be central to a controlled transaction structure because United States federal transfer pricing rules under Internal Revenue Code section 482 apply regardless of the entity's state of organisation.

In operational terms, a Wyoming entity engaged in transactions with controlled domestic or foreign affiliates must apply the federal arm's length principle. The taxpayer must identify controlled transactions, conduct functional analysis, select and apply the best method under Treasury Regulations section 1.482, develop comparable support and maintain contemporaneous documentation to support federal tax reporting and potential penalty protection. The absence of Wyoming state income tax does not eliminate federal tax exposure, foreign tax exposure or state tax exposure in other jurisdictions.

Wyoming's role in group structures is often legal-entity, governance or asset-location driven rather than state income tax driven. A Wyoming holding company may own intellectual property, receive dividends, provide financing, hold investments, employ service providers or enter into agreements with affiliates. Transfer pricing analysis must focus on what that Wyoming entity actually does: its people, decision-making, contractual rights, capital, control of risks, use of assets and ability to perform the functions for which it is compensated. A legal registration address alone does not create economic substance or justify profit allocation.

Wyoming entities generally must maintain good standing through an annual report and annual license tax filed with the Wyoming Secretary of State. The license tax is based on the value of assets located and employed in Wyoming and is the greater of US$60 or US$0.0002 per dollar of Wyoming assets. This state filing requirement is administratively separate from federal transfer pricing. The essential cross-border risk is that a Wyoming entity's activity may establish tax nexus, permanent establishment, income tax, sales tax, gross receipts tax, withholding or reporting obligations in other United States states or foreign countries even though Wyoming itself does not tax corporate income.

Purpose

The purpose of the Wyoming transfer pricing function is to ensure that controlled transactions involving Wyoming entities are arm's length under IRC Section 482, that profit allocation follows actual functions, assets and risks, and that the absence of Wyoming corporate income tax is not mistakenly treated as eliminating federal, state or foreign tax obligations.

It exists to reduce federal and cross-border adjustment risk, support legal entity and economic substance integrity, document intercompany arrangements, identify tax nexus and permanent establishment consequences outside Wyoming and maintain records suitable for IRS and other jurisdictional tax authority review.

Primary Outcome

A defensible Wyoming legal entity and transfer pricing position in which controlled transactions, IRC Section 482 method support, entity functions, people, assets, capital, risk control, intercompany agreements, federal documentation and non-Wyoming state or foreign tax exposure are aligned with the arm's length principle and the actual operating model.

Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies the questions that commonly determine the correct Wyoming transfer pricing and legal entity approach.

  1. Identify whether the Wyoming entity is involved in controlled or related-party transactions and has federal, other-state or foreign tax relevance.
  2. Confirm the entity's tax classification and actual functions, employees, managers, assets, capital, decision-making and control of economically significant risks.
  3. Recognise that Wyoming imposes no state corporate income tax and has no state transfer pricing regime, but that this does not remove federal IRC Section 482 requirements or tax obligations in other jurisdictions.
  4. Determine whether the entity's activities, personnel, assets, customers or contracts create state tax nexus, sales tax, gross receipts tax, withholding or permanent establishment exposure outside Wyoming.
  5. Choose the most appropriate IRC Section 482 method and prepare contemporaneous functional, comparable, financial, legal and substance evidence supporting the Wyoming entity's intercompany remuneration.
  6. Maintain Wyoming annual report and license tax compliance, align federal and other jurisdictional tax returns and consider federal APA or MAP for material recurring cross-border arrangements.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Wyoming Have a State Corporate Income Tax?No. Wyoming does not impose a state corporate income tax. A Wyoming corporation or LLC may still have United States federal income tax and transfer pricing obligations under IRC Section 482, as well as tax obligations in other states or countries where it has activity.
Does Wyoming Have a State Transfer Pricing Regime?No. Wyoming has no separate state corporate income tax transfer pricing regime, no combined reporting system and no state transfer pricing local file or APA programme. Transfer pricing is principally a federal IRC Section 482 and foreign jurisdiction issue for Wyoming entities.
Does a Wyoming Holding Company Eliminate Federal Transfer Pricing Obligations?No. A Wyoming holding company or LLC remains subject to federal IRC Section 482 where it is part of controlled transactions, and its state of organisation does not eliminate the need for arm's length pricing, documentation, economic substance and tax reporting in relevant federal, state or foreign jurisdictions.
What Annual State Payment Applies to Wyoming Entities?Wyoming entities generally file an annual report and pay an annual license tax to the Secretary of State. The fee is the greater of US$60 or US$0.0002 per dollar of assets located and employed in Wyoming.
Does Wyoming Incorporation Create Tax Nexus Only in Wyoming?No. Formation in Wyoming does not determine all tax obligations. A Wyoming entity may create income tax, sales tax, gross receipts tax, withholding, foreign tax or permanent establishment exposure where it conducts business, employs people, owns property, contracts with customers or performs functions outside Wyoming.
Does Wyoming Have a State APA Programme?No. Wyoming has no state APA programme. A taxpayer requiring advance transfer pricing certainty should consider the United States federal APA programme and, for cross-border transactions, potentially bilateral APA or tax treaty MAP processes.
