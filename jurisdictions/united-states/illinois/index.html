Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in Illinois concerns the pricing and Illinois income tax treatment of controlled and intercompany transactions. The arm's length benchmark is principally derived from federal Internal Revenue Code section 482 and Treasury Regulations section 1.482. Illinois-specific consequences are determined by the Illinois Income Tax Act, particularly unitary combined reporting under section 304(e), the definition of a unitary business group in section 1501(a)(27), intercompany transaction elimination rules and related-party interest and intangible expense additions reported through Schedule 80/20.

In operational terms, Illinois requires corporate members of a unitary business group to file a combined return. A unitary business group is a group of persons related through common ownership whose business activities are integrated with, dependent upon and contribute to each other. Combined apportionment treats the group as a unified economic enterprise for Illinois income tax. Income and deductions arising from transactions between group members are eliminated or modified whenever necessary to avoid distortion of the combined base income or apportionment factors.

This produces two analytical layers. First, federal IRC Section 482 analysis remains essential for controlled transactions: functional analysis, best method selection, comparable support and contemporaneous documentation establish the arm's length outcome. Second, Illinois group composition determines whether a transaction is eliminated within the unitary group or remains a payment to a related member outside the group. Transactions outside the Illinois unitary group, including those involving 80/20 companies or other related members, can remain directly relevant to Illinois income and deduction analysis.

Illinois also retains related-party interest and intangible expense addback rules. Schedule 80/20 is used to calculate required additions and statutory exceptions. For taxable years ending on or after December 31, 2025, the exceptions available to unitary filers are materially more limited: the interest exception generally requires proof that the recipient paid the interest to an unrelated person and that the transaction was not principally tax-avoidance motivated and reflects arm's length interest terms, or that adjustments would be unreasonable by clear and convincing evidence, or written agreement by the taxpayer and Department on an alternative apportionment method. Illinois has no separate state APA programme, so federal Section 482 documentation, unitary group records, Schedule 80/20 calculations, agreements and combined apportionment workpapers are essential to a defensible position.

Purpose

The purpose of the Illinois transfer pricing function is to ensure that controlled transactions are arm's length under IRC Section 482 where relevant, that the correct corporations are included in the Illinois unitary combined return, that internal group items are eliminated or modified correctly and that payments to related members outside the group are assessed for Schedule 80/20 additions and state income reflection.

It exists to reduce federal and Illinois adjustment risk, support combined-return integrity, manage related-party interest and intangible expense additions, preserve a defensible 80/20 company position and maintain documentation suitable for Illinois Department of Revenue review.

Primary Outcome

A defensible Illinois transfer pricing and income tax position in which controlled transactions, IRC Section 482 method support, unitary group facts, included and excluded affiliates, Schedule 80/20 analysis, intercompany eliminations, combined base income and apportionment are aligned with Illinois law and the actual operating model.

State Characteristics

Jurisdiction characteristics matter because Illinois is a United States state with mandatory unitary combined reporting for corporate members of a unitary business group. Illinois combines the federal arm's length benchmark with state-specific treatment of combined base income, intercompany eliminations, related-party interest and intangible expense additions and Schedule 80/20 compliance. The state has also adopted a Finnigan-style combined sales factor approach for tax years ending on or after December 31, 2025.

Operational CultureIllinois practice is combined-report and related-party addition focused, combining federal IRC Section 482 analysis with unitary business, Schedule 80/20, intercompany elimination, combined apportionment and documentation review.

Commercial ContextIllinois is a major centre for manufacturing, logistics, transportation, agriculture, food, consumer products, financial services, healthcare, technology, professional services and Midwest United States group operations.
State-Specific FeatureIllinois combined reporting generally eliminates or modifies internal unitary-group transactions, while Schedule 80/20 can require additions for related-party interest and intangible expenses paid to related members outside the relevant group.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Illinois transfer pricing work usually progresses from entity and transaction mapping to unitary combined-report analysis, Schedule 80/20 review and potential Department of Revenue engagement. It matters because Illinois combines federal arm's length analysis with state-specific related-party additions and combined apportionment.

1. Entity, Ownership and Nexus MappingIdentify legal entities, Illinois nexus, ownership relationships, foreign affiliates, related members, 80/20 companies and group structure relevant to Illinois income tax.
2. Unitary Business Group AssessmentDetermine whether entities are related through common ownership and whether their business activities are integrated with, dependent upon and contribute to one another for Illinois unitary group purposes.
3. Intercompany Transaction MappingIdentify goods, services, royalties, financing, IP, management fees, interest and other intercompany transactions, distinguishing dealings among included unitary members from payments to related members outside the group.
4. IRC Section 482 Functional and Method AnalysisConduct functional analysis, select the best method, test comparables and maintain federal arm's length documentation for controlled transactions relevant to federal and Illinois review.
5. Illinois Combined Income and Schedule 80/20 ReviewEliminate or modify internal unitary-group transactions, analyse related-party interest and intangible expense additions and document any limited Schedule 80/20 exceptions for payments outside the group.
6. Combined Return and Apportionment AlignmentPrepare the Illinois combined return, unitary group schedules, Schedule 80/20, combined base income, Finnigan sales factor calculations where applicable and reconciliations to federal tax positions.
7. IDOR Audit, Federal APA or Controversy RouteIf reviewed, provide ownership, unitary business, Schedule 80/20, agreement, financial, Section 482 and federal APA materials; coordinate federal and Illinois controversy procedures as required.
Typical OutputsFederal IRC Section 482 study, functional analysis, benchmarking, Illinois unitary group map, intercompany transaction matrix, Schedule 80/20 support, combined-return workpapers, apportionment schedules, legal agreements and audit response package.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies the questions that commonly determine the correct Illinois transfer pricing and income tax approach.

  1. Identify whether the transaction is between controlled or related parties and has federal or Illinois income tax relevance.
  2. Determine whether the relevant entities are related through common ownership and engaged in an integrated, dependent and contributing unitary business for Illinois purposes.
  3. Identify whether both transaction parties are included in the Illinois unitary business group, whether either is an 80/20 company or whether one party is outside the group.
  4. For included members, identify the income and deduction items that must be eliminated or modified to avoid distortion of combined base income or apportionment factors.
  5. For payments to related members outside the group, conduct and maintain robust IRC Section 482 functional analysis, method selection, comparable support and Schedule 80/20 addback or exception evidence.
  6. Reconcile the federal study, Illinois unitary group, combined return, Schedule 80/20, combined apportionment and any Finnigan sales factor effects before filing and throughout IDOR examination readiness.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Illinois Apply IRC Section 482 Principles to Related-Party Transfer Pricing?Yes. Illinois may reallocate income among related entities using an IRC Section 482-type authority. Federal Section 482 analysis and documentation are therefore important evidence, particularly for transactions with related members outside the Illinois unitary combined group.
Does Illinois Require Unitary Combined Reporting?Yes. Corporate members of a unitary business group are required to file Illinois combined returns under Illinois Income Tax Act section 304(e) and related regulations, subject to specific entity eligibility rules.
Are Intercompany Transactions Eliminated in an Illinois Combined Return?Yes. Income and deductions from transactions between unitary group members are eliminated or modified as necessary to avoid distortion of combined base income and apportionment factors.
What Is Illinois Schedule 80/20?Schedule 80/20 is used to calculate related-party interest and intangible expense additions to Illinois income and applicable exceptions. For taxable years ending on or after December 31, 2025, the scope of exceptions for unitary filers is substantially limited.
Does Illinois Have a Separate State Transfer Pricing Local File?No. Illinois has no standalone state local-file or APA programme. Taxpayers should retain federal IRC Section 482 documentation, unitary group and combined return workpapers, Schedule 80/20 support, intercompany agreements and apportionment evidence for Illinois Department of Revenue review.
Why Are Payments to Related Members Outside the Unitary Group Important?Those payments are not eliminated inside the Illinois combined return and can trigger direct arm's length, related-party addback and Schedule 80/20 analysis, especially for interest and intangible expenses.
