Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in Delaware concerns the pricing and Delaware corporate income tax treatment of controlled and related-party transactions. The core arm's length benchmark is United States federal Internal Revenue Code section 482 and Treasury Regulations section 1.482. Delaware has a corporate income tax and generally uses separate entity reporting rather than mandatory unitary combined reporting, meaning related-party royalties, services, financing, intellectual property charges and other controlled transactions can directly affect the Delaware taxpayer's own income and deductions.

In operational terms, every domestic or foreign corporation doing business in Delaware, unless specifically exempt, generally files Form CIT-TAX and pays Delaware corporate income tax at 8.7% on federal taxable income allocated and apportioned to Delaware. Because Delaware taxable income begins with the federal tax base, federal Section 482 adjustments, functional analysis, method selection, comparable support and contemporaneous documentation are directly relevant to the Delaware position.

Delaware's corporate and business law profile makes intellectual property, holding, financing and legal entity structures especially important in practice. A Delaware corporation may hold trademarks, software, patents, shares, financing assets or group investments and receive royalties, dividends, interest, service income or other related-party payments. Transfer pricing analysis must focus on what that entity actually does: its people, decision-making, contractual rights, capital, control of risks, use of assets and ability to perform the functions for which it is compensated. Incorporation in Delaware alone does not justify intercompany profit allocation.

Delaware corporate franchise tax and Delaware corporate income tax are separate obligations. A Delaware corporation may owe annual franchise tax to the Delaware Division of Corporations regardless of income tax liability and may separately owe Delaware income tax where it does business or derives income from Delaware sources. Delaware has no state transfer pricing local file, master file or APA programme. Federal Section 482 documentation, intercompany agreements, legal entity financials, Delaware tax return workpapers and allocation or apportionment records are therefore central to a defensible Delaware state tax position.

Purpose

The purpose of the Delaware transfer pricing function is to ensure that controlled and related-party transactions are arm's length under IRC Section 482 where relevant, correctly reflected in Delaware separate entity taxable income and supported by evidence of real functions, assets, risks and contractual arrangements.

It exists to reduce federal and Delaware adjustment risk, support deductibility and income recognition for related-party charges, preserve a defensible intellectual property or holding company position, coordinate corporate income and franchise tax obligations and maintain documentation suitable for Delaware Division of Revenue examination.

Primary Outcome

A defensible Delaware transfer pricing and corporate income tax position in which controlled transactions, IRC Section 482 method support, legal entity financial results, related-party royalties, services, financing, IP income, Delaware allocation or apportionment and franchise tax obligations are aligned with the arm's length principle and the actual operating model.

Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies the questions that commonly determine the correct Delaware transfer pricing and corporate tax approach.

  1. Identify whether the Delaware entity is involved in controlled or related-party transactions and has federal, Delaware, other-state or foreign tax relevance.
  2. Confirm the entity's tax classification and actual functions, employees, managers, assets, capital, decision-making, IP rights and control of economically significant risks.
  3. Determine whether the entity is doing business in Delaware or deriving income from Delaware sources and therefore must file Form CIT-TAX and pay Delaware corporate income tax.
  4. Choose the most appropriate IRC Section 482 method and prepare contemporaneous functional, comparable, financial, legal and substance evidence supporting the Delaware entity's intercompany remuneration.
  5. Determine how related-party royalties, services, financing, goods or IP transactions affect federal taxable income, Delaware allocation or apportionment and the separate entity state tax result.
  6. Maintain annual Delaware franchise tax and corporate good standing compliance separately from income tax, then assess other-state and foreign nexus, permanent establishment and federal APA or MAP options for material recurring transactions.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Delaware Have a Corporate Income Tax?Yes. A domestic or foreign corporation doing business in Delaware generally files Form CIT-TAX and pays Delaware corporate income tax at 8.7% on federal taxable income allocated and apportioned to Delaware, subject to statutory exemptions and adjustments.
Does Delaware Apply Arm's Length Principles to Related-Party Transactions?Yes. Delaware corporate income tax begins with federal taxable income, making federal IRC Section 482 adjustments and documentation directly relevant. Delaware may also require allocation or apportionment methods that fairly reflect Delaware income in related-party structures.
Does Delaware Require Unitary Combined Reporting?No. Delaware generally uses separate entity corporate income tax reporting and does not impose mandatory unitary combined reporting. Related-party royalties, services, financing and IP payments can therefore directly affect the Delaware taxpayer's income and deductions.
Does Delaware Corporate Franchise Tax Replace Income Tax?No. Delaware corporate franchise tax and Delaware corporate income tax are separate obligations. A corporation may owe annual franchise tax to the Division of Corporations and may also owe corporate income tax if it does business or derives income from sources in Delaware.
Does Delaware Have a Separate State Transfer Pricing Local File?No. Delaware has no standalone state local-file or master-file form. Taxpayers should retain federal IRC Section 482 documentation, intercompany agreements, legal entity financials, Delaware Form CIT-TAX workpapers and apportionment support for Division of Revenue review.
Why Is Delaware Entity Substance Important?Delaware incorporation alone does not establish economic substance or justify related-party income. A Delaware entity receiving royalties, financing income or service fees should have actual functions, governance, people, assets, capital and risk control consistent with its arm's length remuneration.
