Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in Texas concerns the pricing and franchise tax treatment of controlled and intercompany transactions that affect Texas taxable margin. The arm's length benchmark originates principally in United States federal Internal Revenue Code section 482 and Treasury Regulations section 1.482. The Texas-specific setting is shaped by Texas Tax Code Chapter 171, which applies a franchise tax measured by taxable margin rather than a conventional corporate net income tax.

In operational terms, Texas does not create a separate state replacement for federal transfer pricing. Taxpayers with controlled transactions should begin with federal IRC Section 482 analysis: identify controlled transactions, conduct functional analysis, select the best method, develop comparable support and retain contemporaneous federal documentation. The Texas state tax question is then how the transaction affects total revenue, deductions, cost of goods sold, compensation, apportionment and taxable margin within the Texas franchise tax combined group.

Texas requires taxable entities that are part of an affiliated group engaged in a unitary business to file a combined group report under Tax Code section 171.1014. The combined group is treated as a single taxable entity. In computing total revenue, receipts received from another member of the same combined group are subtracted. Corresponding payments for cost of goods sold or compensation between combined group members are likewise removed, subject to the statutory requirements. This means that transactions between included Texas combined group members generally do not create a current standalone taxable-margin effect in the same way as transactions with related entities outside the group.

The 80/20 rule is a defining Texas feature for cross-border groups. A taxable entity conducting business outside the United States is excluded from the Texas combined group if 80% or more of its property and payroll are assigned to locations outside the United States. Payments from a Texas combined group member to an affiliated entity outside the combined group may affect the cost-of-goods-sold calculation only if made at arm's length. Texas has no separate state local file or APA programme; federal Section 482 documentation, combined-report workpapers, 80/20 analysis, intercompany agreements and taxable-margin reconciliations are therefore essential to Texas Comptroller audit readiness.

Purpose

The purpose of the Texas transfer pricing function is to ensure that controlled transactions are arm's length under IRC Section 482 where relevant, that the appropriate entities are included in the Texas unitary combined group, and that intercompany revenue, costs, deductions and taxable margin are correctly computed under Texas franchise tax law.

It exists to reduce federal and Texas adjustment risk, support correct combined-report composition, document the 80/20 exclusion where applicable, preserve cost-of-goods-sold deductibility for arm's length payments to excluded affiliates and maintain a defensible position for Texas Comptroller examination.

Primary Outcome

A defensible Texas transfer pricing and franchise tax position in which controlled transactions, IRC Section 482 method support, unitary group facts, included and excluded affiliates, 80/20 status, intercompany receipts and deductions, taxable margin and apportionment are aligned with Texas Tax Code requirements and the actual operating model.

State Characteristics

Jurisdiction characteristics matter because Texas is a United States state with a franchise tax measured by taxable margin rather than a conventional state corporate net income tax. Texas uses mandatory unitary combined reporting for affiliated groups and treats the combined group as a single taxable entity. Consequently, the treatment of intercompany transactions is driven by total revenue, cost deductions, group perimeter and apportionment as well as the conventional federal arm's length analysis.

Operational CultureTexas practice is group-composition and taxable-margin focused, combining federal IRC Section 482 documentation with detailed analysis of unitary business, affiliated group membership, 80/20 exclusions, intercompany revenue and deduction treatment.
Legal Framework OrientationFederal transfer pricing principles provide the arm's length benchmark, while Texas Tax Code Chapter 171 governs the franchise tax, combined group, taxable margin, intercompany eliminations and apportionment.
Commercial ContextTexas is a major global centre for energy, petrochemicals, manufacturing, logistics, technology, data centres, semiconductors, construction, healthcare, retail, private equity and North America supply chains.
State-Specific FeatureTexas combined reporting includes affiliated unitary members even without Texas nexus for total revenue, margin and gross receipts everywhere, subject to exclusions such as the 80/20 foreign entity rule.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Texas transfer pricing work usually progresses from entity and transaction mapping to combined-report analysis, taxable-margin computation and potential Texas Comptroller review. It matters because the Texas outcome depends both on the arm's length character of a transaction and on whether the parties are included in the Texas unitary combined group.

1. Entity, Ownership and Nexus MappingIdentify legal entities, Texas nexus, ownership relationships, foreign affiliates, property and payroll location, taxable entity status and group structure relevant to Texas franchise tax.
2. Unitary Combined Group and 80/20 AssessmentDetermine whether the entities are part of an affiliated group engaged in a unitary business and identify any entity excluded because 80% or more of its property and payroll are outside the United States.
3. Intercompany Transaction MappingIdentify goods, services, royalties, financing, IP, management fee and other intercompany transactions, distinguishing dealings among included members from transactions with excluded affiliates.
4. IRC Section 482 Functional and Method AnalysisConduct functional analysis, select the best method, test comparables and maintain federal arm's length documentation for controlled transactions relevant to federal and Texas review.
5. Texas Taxable Margin TreatmentSubtract intercompany receipts between included members from total revenue, apply corresponding intercompany cost or compensation adjustments, and test arm's length requirements for costs paid to affiliates outside the combined group.
6. Franchise Tax Return and Apportionment AlignmentPrepare the Texas combined report, taxable margin calculation, cost-of-goods-sold or compensation election, Texas receipts factor, no-tax-due threshold analysis and reconciliations to federal and entity financial data.
7. Comptroller Audit, Private Ruling or Federal Controversy RouteIf reviewed, provide ownership, unitary business, 80/20, financial, agreement, Section 482 and taxable-margin records to the Comptroller; coordinate with federal APA or IRS examination as relevant.
Typical OutputsFederal IRC Section 482 study, functional analysis, benchmarking, Texas combined group map, 80/20 analysis, intercompany transaction matrix, taxable-margin workpapers, cost-of-goods-sold support, apportionment schedules, legal agreements and audit response package.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies the questions that commonly determine the correct Texas transfer pricing and franchise tax approach.

  1. Identify whether the transaction is between controlled or affiliated parties and has federal or Texas franchise tax relevance.
  2. Determine whether the relevant entities are members of an affiliated group and engaged in a unitary business for Texas combined-report purposes.
  3. Assess whether any foreign affiliated entity is excluded under the Texas 80/20 rule because 80% or more of its property and payroll are outside the United States.
  4. Identify whether both transaction parties are inside the Texas combined group or whether one party is excluded from the group.
  5. For transactions with excluded affiliates, maintain robust IRC Section 482 functional analysis, method selection, comparable support and proof that any cost-of-goods-sold payment meets the Texas arm's length condition.
  6. For included members, eliminate intercompany revenue and corresponding costs correctly, then reconcile taxable margin, deductions, apportionment and the franchise tax report with federal documentation and entity financials.

Key Takeaways
  • Texas applies federal IRC Section 482 arm's length concepts within a franchise tax system based on taxable margin rather than traditional corporate net income.
  • Affiliated entities engaged in a unitary business must file a Texas combined group report, which treats the group as one taxpayer and subtracts qualifying intercompany revenue and corresponding internal costs.
  • Foreign 80/20 affiliates can be excluded from the Texas combined group, making arm's length support particularly important for payments to those entities, including claimed cost-of-goods-sold deductions.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Texas Apply IRC Section 482 Principles to Transfer Pricing?Texas franchise tax law does not replicate the federal IRC Section 482 regime, but arm's length principles remain relevant for transactions with affiliated entities outside a Texas combined group, including the statutory cost-of-goods-sold deduction rule in Tax Code section 171.1012(l). Federal Section 482 documentation is therefore important evidence.
When Is a Texas Combined Group Report Required?Taxable entities that are part of an affiliated group engaged in a unitary business must file a Texas combined group report under Tax Code section 171.1014. The combined group is treated as a single taxable entity for Texas franchise tax purposes.
Are Transactions Between Texas Combined Group Members Eliminated?Yes. In computing Texas combined group total revenue, receipts received from another member of the combined group are subtracted. Corresponding intercompany cost of goods sold or compensation amounts are also subtracted subject to the statutory conditions.
What Is the Texas 80/20 Rule for Combined Reporting?A taxable entity that conducts business outside the United States is generally excluded from a Texas combined group if 80% or more of its property and payroll are assigned to locations outside the United States, subject to specific statutory rules.
Why Is Arm's Length Pricing Important for Excluded Affiliates?A payment made by a combined group member to an affiliated group member outside the combined group may be subtracted as cost of goods sold only if it is made at arm's length under Tax Code section 171.1012(l).
Does Texas Have a State Transfer Pricing Local File or APA Programme?No. Texas has no separate state local-file or APA programme. Taxpayers should maintain federal IRC Section 482 documentation, Texas combined-report workpapers, unitary business evidence, intercompany agreement and taxable-margin support for Texas Comptroller review.
