Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in Croatia concerns how transactions between associated enterprises are priced and evidenced for profit tax purposes under the comparable market price principle. The core legal basis is Article 13 of the Profit Tax Act, supplemented by Article 40 of the Profit Tax Ordinance. Croatian practice is based on the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines as the principal interpretative framework for method selection, transaction delineation and arm's length analysis.

In operational terms, Croatian taxpayers must prepare and maintain transfer pricing documentation for in-scope associated-party dealings. The Tax Administration does not require the full documentation file to be filed with the annual profit tax return; instead, business dealings between associated enterprises are recognised only where the taxpayer possesses and, upon request, can provide information on associated parties, business relationships, methods used, method-selection rationale and transfer price adjustments.

Croatia is distinguished by a separate annual disclosure: taxpayers file the PD-IPO form with the corporate income tax return, providing an overview of related-party transactions. The documentation obligation applies to foreign related-party transactions and to domestic related-party transactions where one party benefits from a privileged tax position, including a lower tax rate or carried-forward tax losses. There is no general size threshold that removes the requirement to prepare support for in-scope dealings.

Croatia also provides advance certainty through unilateral, bilateral and multilateral APAs under Article 14a of the Profit Tax Act. It applies country-by-country reporting for qualifying multinational groups and provides treaty mechanisms for dispute prevention and resolution. This makes Croatian transfer pricing especially relevant for multinational groups operating manufacturing, tourism, maritime, logistics, technology, shared-services, distribution and Adriatic or Central European regional structures.

Purpose

The purpose of the transfer pricing function is to ensure that associated-enterprise transactions connected to Croatia are priced consistently with comparable market prices and can be explained through credible facts, analysis, documentation and the PD-IPO annual disclosure.

It exists to reduce profit-tax adjustment risk, support annual related-party reporting integrity, create a workable evidentiary position when requested by the Tax Administration and support effective APA or MAP planning for cross-border arrangements.

Primary Outcome

A defensible Croatian transfer pricing position in which the relevant associated-enterprise transactions, method, comparable support, documentation file, PD-IPO disclosure, CbCR position and cross-border coordination are aligned with comparable market prices and the actual conduct of the parties.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Croatian transfer pricing work usually progresses from transaction mapping to documentation, PD-IPO disclosure and possible controversy management. It matters because the detailed file is taxpayer-held but the annual related-party transaction overview is filed with the corporate income tax return.

1. Transaction MappingIdentify the associated-enterprise transactions connected to Croatia, including goods, services, financing, licensing, manufacturing, distribution and relevant domestic privileged-tax dealings.
2. Scope AssessmentDetermine whether the transaction is foreign related-party dealing or a domestic related-party dealing where one party has a privileged tax position or tax-loss carryforwards.
3. Functional AnalysisAnalyse what each party actually does, uses and assumes in operational and economic terms, including functions, assets and risks.
4. Method Selection and Comparability ReviewChoose the most appropriate OECD-consistent method, identify comparable market conditions and test the Croatian outcome.
5. Documentation BuildPrepare and retain group-level and local taxpayer documentation, agreements, transaction schedules, method support and benchmarking as relevant.
6. PD-IPO and Tax Return FilingFile the PD-IPO form with the annual corporate income tax return, ensuring the disclosure is consistent with the detailed transfer pricing file.
7. Audit, APA or MAP RouteIf uncertainty or controversy arises, provide documentation on Tax Administration request or pursue APA or treaty-based MAP as relevant.
Typical OutputsTransfer pricing documentation, functional analysis, benchmarking, intercompany agreements, PD-IPO form support, CbCR support, audit response papers and APA documentation.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies scope and compliance questions that commonly determine the right Croatian transfer pricing approach.

  1. Identify whether the transaction is with an associated enterprise and has Croatian profit tax relevance.
  2. Determine whether it is a foreign related-party transaction or a domestic transaction involving a privileged tax position or tax-loss carryforwards.
  3. Confirm the actual functions performed, assets used, risks assumed and contractual terms of each party.
  4. Choose the most appropriate method and establish whether reliable comparable market information supports the outcome.
  5. Prepare documentation and ensure the annual PD-IPO disclosure reconciles with the detailed transaction analysis.
  6. Assess CbCR obligations and decide whether unilateral, bilateral or multilateral APA or MAP planning is appropriate for material cross-border dealings.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Croatia Apply the Arm's Length Principle to Related-Party Transactions?Yes. Croatia applies comparable market prices to associated-enterprise transactions through Article 13 of the Profit Tax Act and Article 40 of the Profit Tax Ordinance, using OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines as an interpretative source.
Does Croatia Require Transfer Pricing Documentation?Yes. Croatian taxpayers with in-scope associated-party transactions must prepare and maintain transfer pricing documentation and provide it to the Tax Administration upon request.
Must the Documentation Be Filed with the Corporate Income Tax Return?No. The detailed documentation is not submitted with the annual profit tax return, but a separate PD-IPO form is filed with the return.
Does Croatia Have a General Documentation Threshold?No. There is no general preparation threshold for in-scope Croatian related-party transactions. Documentation applies to foreign related-party dealings and relevant domestic privileged-tax transactions.
What Is the PD-IPO Form?It is an annual form filed with the Croatian corporate income tax return that provides an overview of transactions effected with related parties.
Does Croatia Offer APAs?Yes. Croatia offers unilateral, bilateral and multilateral APAs under Article 14a of the Profit Tax Act and the APA Ordinance.
