Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in Mexico concerns how transactions with related parties are priced and evidenced for Mexican income tax purposes under the arm's length principle. The core framework is contained in the Mexican Income Tax Law (Ley del Impuesto sobre la Renta, LISR), particularly Articles 76, 76-A, 179 and 180. These rules apply to both domestic and cross-border related-party transactions and require taxpayers to support their accumulated income and authorised deductions using arm's length principles.

In operational terms, Mexico has two interlocking documentation layers. First, taxpayers entering into transactions with related parties must prepare and maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation under Article 76, including related-party identification, functional analysis, transaction detail, method selection, comparable data and arm's length adjustments. Second, large qualifying taxpayers must electronically submit OECD BEPS Action 13-style informative returns under Article 76-A: the master file, local file and country-by-country report.

The local file informative return is due by 15 May of the year following the reported fiscal year and is prepared in Spanish. The master file and CbCR informative returns are due by 31 December of the following year. For fiscal year 2025 reporting, the indexed local-file income threshold is MXN 1,940,100,000. The CbCR threshold for fiscal year 2025 is MXN 16,590,975,000 in consolidated group income. These indexed amounts should be verified annually before filing.

Mexico also has a strong APA framework administered by the Tax Administration Service (SAT), including unilateral, bilateral and multilateral procedures. Maquiladora arrangements have particular importance in Mexican transfer pricing: qualifying maquiladoras may use APA or safe harbour mechanisms to address permanent establishment and arm's length questions for foreign principals. This makes Mexican transfer pricing central to USMCA supply chains, manufacturing, automotive, aerospace, electronics, energy, logistics, services, technology and nearshoring investment structures.

Purpose

The purpose of the transfer pricing function is to ensure that related-party transactions connected to Mexico are priced consistently with the arm's length principle and can be explained through credible facts, analysis, contemporaneous documentation and required SAT informative returns.

It exists to reduce Mexican income tax adjustment risk, support deductibility and income recognition, improve SAT audit readiness, manage USMCA and nearshoring supply-chain tax exposure and create a coherent basis for APA, MAP and cross-border profit allocation.

Primary Outcome

A defensible Mexican transfer pricing position in which the relevant related-party transactions, method, comparable support, contemporaneous documentation, Article 76-A local file, master file, CbCR position and cross-border coordination are aligned with the arm's length principle and the actual conduct of the parties.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Mexican transfer pricing work usually progresses from related-party transaction mapping to contemporaneous documentation, SAT informative returns and possible APA or controversy management. It matters because Mexico applies a universal documentation duty alongside a separate Action 13 filing regime for qualifying taxpayers.

1. Related-Party Transaction MappingIdentify domestic and foreign related-party transactions connected to Mexico, including goods, services, financing, licensing, manufacturing, distribution, maquiladora and permanent establishment arrangements.
2. Universal Documentation AssessmentConfirm the Article 76 obligation to prepare and maintain contemporaneous support for all relevant related-party income, deductions, functions, assets, risks, methods and comparables.
3. Article 76-A Threshold AssessmentAssess whether indexed income thresholds, stock market status, permanent establishment status or other Article 76-A criteria trigger electronic local-file, master-file or CbCR informative returns.
4. Functional Analysis and Method SelectionAnalyse actual functions, assets, risks, contractual terms, manufacturing profile, IP contribution and commercial circumstances, then select the most appropriate method.
5. Documentation and Local File BuildPrepare Article 76 documentation and, if required, the Spanish-language local file informative return with transaction-level functional, comparable and financial support.
6. Master File, CbCR and Return FilingFile the local file by 15 May and master file or CbCR by 31 December of the following year; align the annual tax return and other SAT informative returns with the underlying analysis.
7. SAT Audit, APA, Safe Harbour or MAP RouteIf uncertainty or controversy arises, respond to SAT information requests or pursue APA, maquiladora safe harbour, bilateral procedure or treaty MAP as relevant.
Typical OutputsContemporaneous transfer pricing study, local file, master file, CbCR, SAT informative returns, functional analysis, benchmarking, intercompany agreements, maquiladora support, audit response papers and APA documentation.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine the right Mexican transfer pricing approach.

  1. Identify whether the transaction is with a domestic or foreign related party and has Mexican income tax relevance.
  2. Confirm the actual functions, assets, risks, contractual terms and commercial profile of each party, including whether the Mexican entity is a maquiladora, contract manufacturer, distributor or service provider.
  3. Prepare universal contemporaneous Article 76 documentation supporting the arm's length nature of related-party income and deductions.
  4. Assess Article 76-A filing triggers, including the annually indexed income threshold, permanent establishment status, listed-company status and CbCR group revenue threshold.
  5. Choose the most appropriate method and complete local file support in Spanish for the 15 May filing deadline, where applicable.
  6. File master file and CbCR by 31 December where required, then consider APA, maquiladora safe harbour or MAP for material recurring or double-taxation-sensitive arrangements.

Operating Constraints & Risks

Operating constraints identify the recurring friction points that affect transfer pricing execution in Mexico.

Universal Documentation RiskTaxpayers may wrongly focus only on Article 76-A thresholds and overlook the broader Article 76 requirement to maintain arm's length documentation for domestic and foreign related-party transactions.
Split-Deadline RiskThe Mexican local file deadline of 15 May is earlier than the 31 December master-file and CbCR deadline, requiring separate calendar controls and earlier local data completion.
Spanish-Language RiskThe local file informative return must be prepared in Spanish, creating a translation, terminology and governance risk where group documentation is maintained only in English.
Indexed Threshold RiskArticle 76-A income and CbCR thresholds are indexed annually, so taxpayers must verify the threshold applicable to the reporting fiscal year rather than relying on outdated amounts.
Maquiladora and Supply-Chain RiskManufacturing and maquiladora structures require careful analysis of functional profile, safe harbour or APA eligibility, foreign-principal permanent establishment risk and consistency with USMCA supply-chain reality.
