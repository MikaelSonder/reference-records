Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in Bulgaria concerns how transactions between related parties are priced and evidenced for corporate income tax purposes under the arm's length principle. The core substantive rule is Article 15 of the Corporate Income Tax Act (CITA), which requires related-party transactions to be priced as they would be between independent parties. The procedural and definitional framework is contained in the Tax and Social Security Procedure Code (TSSPC), supported by transfer pricing regulations and OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

In operational terms, Bulgaria has a dual documentation environment. All taxpayers may be required under the general tax-procedure rules to demonstrate the arm's length nature of related-party prices during a National Revenue Agency (NRA) review. In addition, enhanced mandatory Action 13-style documentation applies to qualifying Bulgarian corporate taxpayers and Bulgarian permanent establishments with covered cross-border related-party transactions: the local file is prepared annually and the master file is required where the entity belongs to a multinational group.

The mandatory documentation regime is generally triggered where, at 31 December of the preceding year, a Bulgarian taxpayer or permanent establishment has balance-sheet assets exceeding BGN 38 million and either net sales exceeding BGN 76 million or an average number of employees exceeding 250. Certain entities and local-only transaction profiles are excluded from the mandatory local-file and master-file obligation, but this does not eliminate the wider obligation to substantiate arm's length pricing if requested in audit.

Bulgaria applies country-by-country reporting for qualifying multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million, equivalent to BGN 1,466,872,500. It does not operate a formal APA programme under current legislation. Tax treaty MAP procedures, the Tax Treaty Directorate of the NRA and, in limited circumstances, non-binding opinions remain relevant for groups seeking to manage international transfer pricing uncertainty.

Purpose

The purpose of the transfer pricing function is to ensure that related-party transactions connected to Bulgaria are priced consistently with the arm's length principle and can be explained through credible facts, analysis and documentation appropriate to the taxpayer's statutory status.

It exists to reduce adjustment risk, support local-file and master-file compliance for qualifying taxpayers, maintain a defensible general evidence position for other taxpayers and create a workable basis for NRA audit, treaty MAP or cross-border dispute management.

Primary Outcome

A defensible Bulgarian transfer pricing position in which the relevant controlled transactions, method, comparable support, local file or master file status, CbCR position and cross-border coordination are aligned with the arm's length principle and the actual conduct of the parties.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics matter because Bulgarian transfer pricing combines a broad audit-time evidence obligation with a formal two-tier Action 13-style documentation regime for qualifying large cross-border taxpayers. The Bulgarian setting gives practical importance to accurate statutory size testing, documentation availability, local financial support and consistent use of OECD methods in an NRA audit environment.

Operational CultureBulgarian practice combines statutory documentation compliance for qualifying taxpayers with a broad expectation that all taxpayers can substantiate arm's length pricing in audit.
Legal Framework OrientationThe arm's length principle is embedded in the Corporate Income Tax Act and procedural rules, with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines used as an important methodological reference.
Commercial ContextBulgaria is frequently used for IT and business-process services, manufacturing, logistics, distribution, shared services, energy and regional Southeast European operating structures.
Certainty EnvironmentBulgaria has tax treaty MAP and competent-authority mechanisms but does not currently operate a formal APA programme.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Bulgarian transfer pricing work usually progresses from transaction mapping to documentation readiness and possible controversy management. It matters because qualifying entities have formal documentation deadlines, while all taxpayers need an evidentiary position that can be produced during a National Revenue Agency audit.

1. Transaction MappingIdentify the related-party transactions connected to Bulgaria, including goods, services, financing, licensing, manufacturing, distribution and permanent establishment dealings.
2. Size-Criteria and Scope AssessmentReview prior-year assets, net sales and employee count, then determine whether the enhanced local-file and master-file regime applies to covered cross-border transactions.
3. Functional AnalysisAnalyse what each party actually does, controls and assumes in operational and economic terms.
4. Method Selection and Comparability ReviewChoose the most appropriate method under Bulgarian methodology and OECD-based practice, then prepare comparable support for the result.
5. Local File and Master File BuildPrepare the local file annually where mandatory and coordinate the master file where the Bulgarian entity forms part of a multinational group.
6. Tax Return and CbCR MonitoringAlign the Bulgarian corporate income tax position, documentation, financial records and any CbCR notification or filing obligation.
7. NRA Audit or MAP RouteRespond to an NRA request with documentation and evidence, or consider treaty MAP for cross-border double-taxation issues where no APA option is available.
Typical OutputsLocal file, master file, functional analysis, benchmarking, intercompany alignment notes, CbCR support, audit response papers and MAP documentation.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine the right Bulgarian transfer pricing approach.

  1. Identify whether the transaction is with a related party and has Bulgarian corporate income tax relevance.
  2. Confirm the parties, transaction categories, actual functions, assets and economically significant risks.
  3. Assess whether the taxpayer met the preceding-year statutory size criteria for enhanced mandatory documentation.
  4. Determine whether the transaction is a covered cross-border related-party transaction and whether any exemption applies.
  5. Choose the most appropriate method and establish reliable comparable or other arm's length support.
  6. Prepare local file or master file where required, retain general evidence in all cases, assess CbCR status and consider MAP for material double-taxation risk.

Cross-border relevance is central because Bulgarian transfer pricing primarily concerns related-party dealings across tax boundaries and the allocation of profit within international groups. Bulgaria's framework connects domestic rules with OECD methodology, EU obligations, tax treaties, CbCR and MAP, so the Bulgarian position must be consistent with group policy and counterparty-jurisdiction evidence.

Key Takeaways
  • Bulgaria applies the arm's length principle through Article 15 of the Corporate Income Tax Act and OECD-informed transfer pricing methodology.
  • Qualifying large cross-border taxpayers prepare annual local file and, where applicable, master file documentation, while a general evidence obligation applies more broadly in NRA audits.
  • Bulgaria applies CbCR to qualifying multinational groups but does not currently offer a formal APA programme; MAP is the principal treaty mechanism for double-taxation disputes.
Operating Constraints & Risks

Operating constraints identify the recurring friction points that affect transfer pricing execution in Bulgaria.

Size-Criteria RiskIncorrect assessment of prior-year asset, revenue or employee thresholds can cause a qualifying entity to miss mandatory local-file or master-file requirements.
General Evidence RiskTaxpayers outside the enhanced mandatory documentation regime may incorrectly assume that no support is required, despite the broad NRA audit-time obligation to demonstrate arm's length pricing.
Contemporaneous Documentation RiskLate or reconstructed documentation can be less persuasive in audit and may not meet statutory preparation timetables for qualifying entities.
Comparable and Method RiskWeak benchmarking, failure to follow the relevant method framework or inconsistent functional characterisation can undermine the Bulgarian arm's length conclusion.
Certainty Limitation RiskThe absence of a formal APA programme means groups must rely on documentation, audit defence, treaty MAP or non-binding opinions rather than advance binding pricing certainty.

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Bulgaria Apply the Arm's Length Principle to Related-Party Transactions?Yes. Bulgaria applies the arm's length principle under Article 15 of the Corporate Income Tax Act, with methodology governed through the Tax and Social Security Procedure Code and applicable transfer pricing regulations.
Does Bulgaria Require Local File and Master File Documentation?Yes. Qualifying Bulgarian corporate taxpayers and permanent establishments with covered cross-border related-party transactions must prepare annual local file documentation and, when part of a multinational group, a master file.
Which Taxpayers Are Subject to Mandatory Documentation?The mandatory regime applies where the taxpayer meets statutory preceding-year size criteria and has covered cross-border related-party transactions, subject to specific exemptions.
