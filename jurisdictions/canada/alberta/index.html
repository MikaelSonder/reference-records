Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in Alberta is governed primarily by Canada's federal transfer pricing regime in section 247 of the Income Tax Act. The rules apply where a Canadian taxpayer or partnership enters into a transaction or series of transactions with a non-resident person with whom it does not deal at arm's length. The Canadian tax outcome must reflect terms and conditions that would have been made between arm's-length persons, using OECD-consistent transfer pricing analysis.

Alberta does not operate a separate provincial transfer pricing code, local file or APA programme. Alberta corporate income tax is administered by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) through the federal-provincial corporate tax collection system. The federal taxable income outcome after any section 247 adjustment is therefore central to Alberta corporate income tax. The Alberta-specific dimension is the province's legal entity operations, resources, project activity, provincial allocation and commercial context, rather than a separate provincial arm's length statute.

Canada operates a contemporaneous documentation system. The taxpayer must make or obtain records and documents on or before the documentation-due date, generally six months after the end of the relevant taxation year or fiscal period. Where CRA makes a written request, contemporaneous documentation must be provided within three months. The documents must describe the property or services, transaction terms, identities and relationships of the parties, functions, risks, assets, comparable information, method selection, assumptions and arm's length analysis.

Canadian reporting is also distinguished by Form T106, Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents. It is generally required where total reportable transactions with all non-arm's-length non-residents exceed CAD 1 million during the taxation year. Canada offers unilateral, bilateral and multilateral APAs and treaty MAP procedures. Alberta transfer pricing is especially relevant for Calgary and wider Alberta energy, oil sands, natural gas, pipelines, power, renewables, mining, engineering, construction, agriculture, technology, financial services, professional services and Canada-United States multinational group structures.

Purpose

The purpose of the Alberta transfer pricing function is to ensure that transactions with non-arm's-length non-residents are priced consistently with the arm's length principle under section 247 and can be explained through credible facts, functional and economic analysis, contemporaneous documentation and Form T106 reporting where applicable.

It exists to reduce Canadian federal and Alberta corporate income tax adjustment risk, support CRA audit readiness, ensure documentation and T106 reporting integrity, accurately reflect Alberta energy, resource, project and service functions and create a coherent basis for unilateral, bilateral or multilateral APA, MAP and cross-border profit allocation.

Primary Outcome

A defensible Alberta transfer pricing and corporate income tax position in which the relevant non-arm's-length non-resident transactions, method, comparable support, contemporaneous documentation, Form T106, Canadian federal taxable income, Alberta corporate income tax consequences and cross-border coordination are aligned with the arm's length principle and actual conduct.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Alberta transfer pricing work usually progresses from transaction mapping to contemporaneous documentation, Form T106 reporting and potential CRA engagement. It matters because Canada imposes a federal transaction-level arm's length standard and defined documentation timetable that informs Alberta corporate income tax consequences.

1. Non-Resident Related-Party Transaction MappingIdentify transactions and arrangements between the Alberta taxpayer and non-resident non-arm's-length persons, including goods, services, financing, licensing, IP, management fees, resource marketing and permanent establishment dealings.
2. Reporting and Documentation AssessmentAssess total reportable non-resident transactions for Form T106, determine the documentation-due date and identify CbCR, master-file or other group reporting obligations where applicable.
3. Functional AnalysisAnalyse actual Alberta, Canadian and foreign functions, assets, risks, contractual terms, decision-making, energy or resource project operations, economic circumstances and value creation.
4. Method Selection and Comparability ReviewChoose the most appropriate OECD-consistent method, identify internal or external comparables, apply adjustments where appropriate and test the Canadian arm's length outcome.
5. Contemporaneous Documentation BuildMake or obtain required section 247 records and documents on or before the documentation-due date, generally six months after year-end, and retain agreements, financial evidence and analysis.
6. T2 Return, T106 and Alberta Tax AlignmentReflect the arm's length outcome in the T2 return, file Form T106 where required and reconcile federal taxable income and adjustments with Alberta corporate income tax computations.
7. CRA Audit, APA or MAP RouteIf uncertainty or controversy arises, provide contemporaneous documentation within three months after a CRA written request or pursue unilateral, bilateral or multilateral APA or treaty MAP as relevant.
Typical OutputsContemporaneous documentation, functional analysis, benchmarking, Form T106, intercompany agreements, financing study, resource or project support, T2 return support, Alberta tax reconciliation, CbCR support, CRA audit response papers, APA documentation and MAP materials.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies the threshold and approach questions that commonly determine the right Alberta transfer pricing approach.

  1. Identify whether the transaction or arrangement is with a non-resident person with whom the Alberta taxpayer or partnership does not deal at arm's length.
  2. Confirm the actual functions, assets, risks, contractual terms, decision-making, Alberta energy or resource operations and foreign counterpart contribution for each transaction.
  3. Determine the documentation-due date, generally six months after the Alberta taxpayer's taxation year or fiscal period, and prepare contemporaneous records before that date.
  4. Aggregate reportable transactions with all non-arm's-length non-residents and assess whether Form T106 is required above CAD 1 million.
  5. Choose the most appropriate OECD-consistent method and prepare functional, comparable, financial and legal support for the Canadian arm's length result.
  6. Assess the effect on federal taxable income and Alberta corporate income tax, then consider CbCR, unilateral, bilateral or multilateral APA or MAP for material recurring or double-taxation-sensitive arrangements.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Canada Apply the Arm's Length Principle to Alberta Companies?Yes. Canada's Income Tax Act section 247 applies to transactions between a Canadian taxpayer or partnership and a non-resident with whom it does not deal at arm's length. The resulting federal taxable income also informs Alberta corporate income tax administered by the Canada Revenue Agency.
Does Alberta Have a Separate Transfer Pricing Regime?No. Alberta does not operate a separate provincial transfer pricing code, local file or APA programme. Alberta corporate income tax is administered through the federal CRA system, so federal section 247 analysis, documentation and adjustments flow into the Alberta corporate income tax computation.
When Is Canadian Contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation Due?Contemporaneous documentation must be made or obtained on or before the taxpayer's documentation-due date, which is generally six months after the end of the relevant taxation year or fiscal period. It must be provided to CRA within three months after a written request.
When Is Form T106 Required?Form T106, Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents, is generally required when total reportable transactions with all non-resident persons with whom the taxpayer does not deal at arm's length exceed CAD 1 million in the taxation year.
What Is the Canadian Transfer Pricing Penalty?Canada may impose a penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment where the statutory threshold is exceeded and the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to determine and use arm's length prices or allocations, including adequate contemporaneous documentation.
Does Canada Offer APAs for Alberta Businesses?Yes. The Canada Revenue Agency administers unilateral, bilateral and multilateral advance pricing arrangements for Canadian taxpayers, including Alberta businesses. Bilateral and multilateral APAs are negotiated with treaty partner competent authorities.
