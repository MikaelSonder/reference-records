Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in British Columbia is governed primarily by Canada's federal transfer pricing regime in section 247 of the Income Tax Act. The rules apply where a Canadian taxpayer or partnership enters into a transaction or series of transactions with a non-resident person with whom it does not deal at arm's length. The Canadian tax outcome must reflect terms and conditions that would have been made between arm's-length persons, using OECD-consistent transfer pricing analysis.

British Columbia does not operate a separate provincial transfer pricing code, local file or APA programme. British Columbia corporate income tax is administered by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) through the federal-provincial corporate tax collection system. The federal taxable income outcome after any section 247 adjustment is therefore central to the British Columbia corporate income tax computation. The British Columbia-specific dimension is the province's legal entity operations, provincial allocation and commercial context, rather than a separate provincial arm's length statute.

Canada operates a contemporaneous documentation system. The taxpayer must make or obtain records and documents on or before the documentation-due date, generally six months after the end of the relevant taxation year or fiscal period. Where CRA makes a written request, contemporaneous documentation must be provided within three months. The documents must describe the property or services, transaction terms, identities and relationships of the parties, functions, risks, assets, comparable information, method selection, assumptions and arm's length analysis.

Canadian reporting is also distinguished by Form T106, Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents. It is generally required where total reportable transactions with all non-arm's-length non-residents exceed CAD 1 million during the taxation year. Canada offers unilateral, bilateral and multilateral APAs and treaty MAP procedures. British Columbia transfer pricing is especially relevant for Vancouver and wider British Columbia technology, software, artificial intelligence, film and digital media, mining, forestry, energy, natural resources, clean technology, professional services, logistics, ports, Asia-Pacific trade and Canada-United States multinational group structures.

Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies the threshold and approach questions that commonly determine the right British Columbia transfer pricing approach.

  1. Identify whether the transaction or arrangement is with a non-resident person with whom the British Columbia taxpayer or partnership does not deal at arm's length.
  2. Confirm the actual functions, assets, risks, contractual terms, decision-making, British Columbia operations, resource or technology contribution and foreign counterpart contribution for each transaction.
  3. Determine the documentation-due date, generally six months after the British Columbia taxpayer's taxation year or fiscal period, and prepare contemporaneous records before that date.
  4. Aggregate reportable transactions with all non-arm's-length non-residents and assess whether Form T106 is required above CAD 1 million.
  5. Choose the most appropriate OECD-consistent method and prepare functional, comparable, financial and legal support for the Canadian arm's length result.
  6. Assess the effect on federal taxable income and British Columbia corporate income tax, then consider CbCR, unilateral, bilateral or multilateral APA or MAP for material recurring or double-taxation-sensitive arrangements.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Canada Apply the Arm's Length Principle to British Columbia Companies?Yes. Canada's Income Tax Act section 247 applies to transactions between a Canadian taxpayer or partnership and a non-resident with whom it does not deal at arm's length. The resulting federal taxable income also informs British Columbia corporate income tax administered by the Canada Revenue Agency.
Does British Columbia Have a Separate Transfer Pricing Regime?No. British Columbia does not operate a separate provincial transfer pricing code, local file or APA programme. British Columbia corporate income tax is administered through the federal CRA system, so federal section 247 analysis, documentation and adjustments flow into the British Columbia corporate income tax computation.
When Is Canadian Contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation Due?Contemporaneous documentation must be made or obtained on or before the taxpayer's documentation-due date, which is generally six months after the end of the relevant taxation year or fiscal period. It must be provided to CRA within three months after a written request.
When Is Form T106 Required?Form T106, Information Return of Non-Arm's Length Transactions with Non-Residents, is generally required when total reportable transactions with all non-resident persons with whom the taxpayer does not deal at arm's length exceed CAD 1 million in the taxation year.
What Is the Canadian Transfer Pricing Penalty?Canada may impose a penalty equal to 10% of the net transfer pricing adjustment where the statutory threshold is exceeded and the taxpayer did not make reasonable efforts to determine and use arm's length prices or allocations, including adequate contemporaneous documentation.
Does Canada Offer APAs for British Columbia Businesses?Yes. The Canada Revenue Agency administers unilateral, bilateral and multilateral advance pricing arrangements for Canadian taxpayers, including British Columbia businesses. Bilateral and multilateral APAs are negotiated with treaty partner competent authorities.
