Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in China concerns how related-party transactions are priced and evidenced for enterprise income tax purposes under the arm's length principle. Article 41 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law authorises tax authorities to make reasonable adjustments where related-party arrangements reduce taxable income or taxable profit. The detailed administrative framework is contained principally in State Taxation Administration Announcement No. 42 of 2016 on the administration of related-party transactions and contemporaneous documentation.

In operational terms, China applies a detailed three-file transfer pricing documentation architecture: the master file, local file and special issue file. The local file is triggered where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for transfers of tangible assets, RMB 100 million for transfers of financial assets, RMB 100 million for transfers in ownership of intangible assets, or RMB 40 million for all other transactions, including services, royalties, interest and similar dealings.

The master file is required where the Chinese enterprise has cross-border related-party transactions and the ultimate holding company has prepared a master file, or where the enterprise's total annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. The special issue file applies to qualifying cost sharing arrangements and thin capitalisation situations. Local file and special issue file documentation must be completed by 30 June of the year after the transaction year; the master file must be completed within 12 months after the end of the ultimate holding company's fiscal year. All contemporaneous documentation must be supplied within 30 days after a tax authority request.

China also requires annual Related Party Transaction forms with the enterprise income tax return and applies country-by-country reporting to qualifying multinational groups, generally from the RMB 5.5 billion consolidated revenue threshold. The State Taxation Administration provides unilateral, bilateral and multilateral APA routes, including a simplified unilateral APA programme. This makes Chinese transfer pricing especially material for foreign-invested enterprises, contract manufacturers, distributors, technology and R&D entities, service centres, high-value supply chains and multinational groups operating in or through China.

Purpose

The purpose of the transfer pricing function is to ensure that related-party transactions connected to China are priced consistently with the arm's length principle and can be explained through credible facts, analysis, contemporaneous documentation and required annual reporting.

It exists to reduce enterprise income tax adjustment risk, improve State Taxation Administration audit readiness, support consistent group pricing and create a workable evidentiary position for APA, MAP and cross-border profit-allocation management.

Primary Outcome

A defensible Chinese transfer pricing position in which the relevant related-party transactions, method, comparable support, local file, master file, special issue file, annual related-party transaction forms, CbCR position and cross-border coordination are aligned with the arm's length principle and the actual conduct of the parties.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics matter because Chinese transfer pricing operates in a very large, export-oriented and increasingly sophisticated international tax environment. China is central to global manufacturing, processing, distribution, technology, R&D and supply-chain arrangements. The framework combines detailed transaction-type thresholds, annual related-party reporting, OECD Action 13 documentation, special issue files and a risk-focused tax administration that gives significant attention to losses, low margins, intangible contributions and value creation in China.

Operational CultureChina is documentation- and audit-focused, with strong attention to transaction data, local value creation, functional profiles, comparable support, annual related-party reporting and timely production of contemporaneous files.
Legal Framework OrientationThe arm's length principle is codified in the Enterprise Income Tax Law and applied through detailed State Taxation Administration measures, particularly Announcement 42, with OECD Action 13 architecture adapted to Chinese practice.
Commercial ContextChina is a major global base for manufacturing, processing, contract manufacturing, technology, e-commerce, R&D, distribution, logistics, sourcing and regional supply-chain operations.
China-Specific Documentation FeatureChina requires a special issue file in addition to master file and local file for qualifying cost sharing arrangements and thin capitalisation cases.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Chinese transfer pricing work usually progresses from transaction mapping to annual related-party reporting, contemporaneous documentation and potential tax authority engagement. It matters because China combines detailed transaction-type thresholds with master file, local file, special issue file and annual return disclosure requirements.

1. Related-Party Transaction MappingIdentify related-party transactions connected to China, including tangible goods, services, licensing, financing, contract manufacturing, distribution, R&D, cost sharing and thin capitalisation arrangements.
2. Annual Reporting AssessmentPrepare annual Related Party Transaction forms for filing with the enterprise income tax return and identify the relevant transaction categories and values.
3. Documentation Threshold AssessmentAssess local-file transaction thresholds, master-file conditions, special issue file requirements and any domestic-only transaction exemption.
4. Functional Analysis and Method SelectionAnalyse actual functions, assets, risks and value creation, then choose the most appropriate method and comparable support for the Chinese entity outcome.
5. Local File and Special Issue File BuildPrepare local file and any special issue file by 30 June of the year after the transaction year, including financial, functional and comparable support.
6. Master File and CbCR CoordinationObtain or prepare the master file within 12 months of the ultimate holding company's fiscal year-end and assess CbCR reporting and notification requirements.
7. STA Audit, APA or MAP RouteIf uncertainty or controversy arises, supply contemporaneous documentation within 30 days of request or pursue unilateral, bilateral or multilateral APA or treaty MAP as relevant.
Typical OutputsRelated Party Transaction forms, local file, master file, special issue file, functional analysis, benchmarking, intercompany agreements, CbCR support, audit response papers and APA documentation.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine the right Chinese transfer pricing approach.

  1. Identify whether the transaction is with a related party and has Chinese enterprise income tax relevance.
  2. Classify the transaction as tangible assets, financial assets, intangible assets, services or other transactions, and identify cost sharing or thin capitalisation issues.
  3. Assess whether annual transaction values exceed RMB 200 million for tangible assets, RMB 100 million for financial assets or intangibles, or RMB 40 million for other transactions.
  4. Determine whether the master file is required because the group has cross-border related-party transactions and has prepared a master file, or the entity has annual related-party transactions exceeding RMB 1 billion.
  5. Confirm whether a local file is mandatory regardless of threshold because a foreign-related contract manufacturing, distribution or contract R&D entity records a loss.
  6. Prepare annual reporting and contemporaneous files, assess CbCR obligations and consider simplified unilateral, bilateral or multilateral APA or MAP for material recurring transactions.
