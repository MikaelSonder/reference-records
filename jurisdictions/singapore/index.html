Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in Singapore concerns how related-party transactions are priced and evidenced for Singapore income tax purposes under the arm's length principle. Section 34D of the Income Tax Act 1947 authorises the Inland Revenue Authority of Singapore (IRAS) to adjust Singapore taxable profit upward, or reduce a loss, where related-party conditions are not arm's length.

In operational terms, Singapore applies a contemporary, OECD-aligned documentation framework under Section 34F and the Income Tax (Transfer Pricing Documentation) Rules 2018. Taxpayers must prepare contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where gross revenue derived from trade or business exceeds S$10 million for the basis period, or where documentation was required for the immediately preceding basis period, unless a transaction-specific exemption applies. This continuing-obligation feature means the documentation requirement can remain in force after the initial revenue trigger.

For Year of Assessment 2026 and later, transaction-specific documentation exemptions generally apply where purchases or sales of goods do not exceed S$15 million per category per financial year, loans granted or received do not exceed S$15 million in outstanding balance, and most other transaction categories—including services, royalties, licences, guarantees and similar dealings—do not exceed S$2 million per category. The detailed documentation is completed by the income tax return filing due date, not filed with the return, and must be produced to IRAS within 30 days of a request.

Singapore also requires the Related Party Transactions Form (RPT Form) where related-party transactions disclosed in the accounts exceed S$15 million. IRAS imposes a 5% surcharge on transfer pricing adjustments made under Section 34D from YA 2019. Singapore offers bilateral and multilateral APAs, but not unilateral APAs, and has a mature MAP framework. This makes Singapore transfer pricing important for regional headquarters, finance, treasury, technology, trading, logistics, services and Asia-Pacific group structures.

Purpose

The purpose of the transfer pricing function is to ensure that related-party transactions connected to Singapore are priced consistently with the arm's length principle and can be explained through credible contemporaneous facts, analysis, documentation and RPT reporting where applicable.

It exists to reduce adjustment and surcharge risk, improve IRAS audit readiness, support consistent group pricing and create a coherent evidentiary basis for bilateral or multilateral APA, MAP and cross-border profit-allocation management.

Primary Outcome

A defensible Singapore transfer pricing position in which the relevant related-party transactions, method, comparable support, contemporaneous documentation, RPT Form position and cross-border coordination are aligned with the arm's length principle and the actual conduct of the parties.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how Singapore transfer pricing work usually progresses from related-party transaction mapping to contemporaneous documentation, RPT disclosure and possible audit or dispute prevention. It matters because Singapore combines a revenue trigger, transaction-category exemptions and short document-production timing.

1. Related-Party Transaction MappingIdentify related-party transactions connected to Singapore, including goods, services, financing, licensing, management fees, treasury, IP and regional headquarters dealings.
2. Documentation Trigger AssessmentAssess whether gross revenue from trade or business exceeds S$10 million, whether the continuing documentation rule applies and whether transaction-category exemptions are available.
3. Functional AnalysisAnalyse what each party actually does, controls and assumes in operational and economic terms, including functions, assets, risks, decision-making and intangibles.
4. Method Selection and Comparability ReviewChoose the most appropriate OECD-consistent method, prepare comparable support and test whether the Singapore outcome is arm's length.
5. Contemporaneous Documentation BuildPrepare the prescribed documentation by the income tax return filing due date and retain it with supporting agreements, financial data and benchmarking.
6. RPT Form and Return FilingFile the RPT Form with the corporate income tax return where the disclosed related-party transaction value exceeds S$15 million.
7. IRAS Audit, APA or MAP RouteIf uncertainty or controversy arises, provide documentation within 30 days of an IRAS request or pursue bilateral or multilateral APA or treaty MAP as relevant.
Typical OutputsContemporaneous transfer pricing documentation, functional analysis, benchmarking, intercompany agreements, RPT Form support, audit response papers and APA or MAP documentation.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine the right Singapore transfer pricing approach.

  1. Identify whether the transaction is with a related party and has Singapore income tax relevance.
  2. Confirm which party performs the key functions, controls economically significant risks and contributes relevant assets or intangibles.
  3. Assess whether gross revenue from trade or business exceeds S$10 million or whether documentation was required in the immediately preceding basis period.
  4. Assess whether each related-party transaction category falls below its applicable exemption threshold, including S$15 million for goods or loans and S$2 million for most other categories from YA 2026.
  5. Determine whether the RPT Form is required because related-party transaction values disclosed in the accounts exceed S$15 million.
  6. Choose the most appropriate method, prepare contemporaneous support and consider bilateral or multilateral APA or MAP for material recurring cross-border arrangements.

Operating Constraints & Risks

Operating constraints identify the recurring friction points that affect transfer pricing execution in Singapore.

Continuing Documentation RiskOnce documentation is required, the obligation may continue into the following basis period even if revenue falls, unless the applicable conditions for exiting the requirement are satisfied.
Transaction-Category RiskDocumentation exemptions apply separately by transaction category. Aggregating unlike transactions or applying the wrong threshold can lead to an incorrect exemption conclusion.
30-Day Production RiskDocumentation is not filed with the annual return but must be submitted to IRAS within 30 days of request, requiring contemporaneous preparation and document control.
RPT Reporting RiskThe RPT Form must be completed when the S$15 million disclosure threshold is exceeded and should reconcile with accounts and transfer pricing documentation.
Surcharge ExposureIRAS imposes a 5% surcharge on the amount of a transfer pricing adjustment made under Section 34D, in addition to potential tax, interest and penalty consequences.

FAQ

The FAQ section collects recurring threshold questions in a concise handbook format.

Does Singapore Apply the Arm's Length Principle to Related-Party Transactions?Yes. Singapore applies the arm's length principle under Section 34D of the Income Tax Act 1947. IRAS may adjust Singapore taxable profit or loss where related-party pricing is not arm's length.
Who Must Prepare Singapore Transfer Pricing Documentation?Taxpayers must prepare documentation if gross revenue from trade or business exceeds S$10 million for the basis period, or if documentation was required for the immediately preceding basis period, unless a transaction-specific exemption applies.
When Is Documentation Completed and Submitted?Documentation must be completed by the income tax return filing due date. It is not filed with the return but must be submitted to IRAS within 30 days of a request.
What Transaction Thresholds Apply from YA 2026?Goods purchases and sales each have a S$15 million annual threshold; loans have a S$15 million outstanding-balance threshold; most other transaction categories have a S$2 million annual threshold.
What Is the RPT Form Threshold?A company must complete the Related Party Transactions Form where the value of related-party transactions disclosed in its accounts exceeds S$15 million for the financial year.
Does Singapore Offer Unilateral APAs?No. Singapore's APA programme is limited to bilateral and multilateral APAs with treaty partners; unilateral APAs are not offered.
