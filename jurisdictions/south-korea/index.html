Executive Summary

Transfer pricing in South Korea concerns how overseas related-party transactions are priced and evidenced for Korean tax purposes under the arm's length principle. The framework is principally governed by the Adjustment of International Taxes Act (AITA), together with the Enforcement Decree and Enforcement Rule of the AITA. It provides the legal basis for arm's length pricing, method selection, corresponding adjustments, documentation, APAs and mutual agreement procedures.

In operational terms, South Korea applies the OECD BEPS Action 13 three-tier documentation framework through the Combined Report of International Transaction Information, commonly called CRIT. CRIT comprises a master file, a local file and, where applicable, a country-by-country report. A Korean corporation or Korean branch is generally required to submit master file and local file documentation when both annual gross sales exceed KRW 100 billion and aggregate overseas related-party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion in the relevant fiscal year.

The CRIT submission deadline is generally 12 months from the end of the month in which the fiscal year ends. Country-by-country reporting applies, among others, where the Korean ultimate parent has prior-year consolidated group revenue exceeding KRW 1 trillion. The CbCR notification is filed within six months of the Korean entity's fiscal year-end. Taxpayers below the CRIT thresholds are not outside transfer pricing compliance: they must maintain reasonable contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation and provide it upon National Tax Service request.

South Korea also offers unilateral, bilateral and multilateral APAs administered by the National Tax Service (NTS). The APA system includes the possibility of rollback within the statutory framework and is especially important for Korean groups and foreign multinationals with material recurring manufacturing, technology, R&D, distribution, financing or intangible-related transactions. The result is a highly structured transfer pricing environment in which threshold testing, filing discipline, Korean-language readiness and cross-border consistency are central.

Purpose

The purpose of the transfer pricing function is to ensure that overseas related-party transactions connected to South Korea are priced consistently with the arm's length principle and can be explained through credible facts, analysis, CRIT reporting and contemporaneous documentation.

It exists to reduce adjustment risk, support filing integrity, improve NTS audit readiness, create a coherent evidentiary basis for cross-border profit allocation and support effective APA or MAP planning where required.

Primary Outcome

A defensible Korean transfer pricing position in which the relevant overseas related-party transactions, method, comparable support, CRIT, master file, local file, CbCR position and cross-border coordination are aligned with the arm's length principle and the actual conduct of the parties.

Country Characteristics

Country characteristics matter because South Korean transfer pricing operates in a formal, technically developed and export-oriented international tax environment. South Korea's industrial, automotive, electronics, technology, semiconductor, R&D and manufacturing economy produces material cross-border controlled transactions, while the NTS applies a structured Action 13 reporting, audit and APA framework. The Korean system gives particular weight to the CRIT package, dual threshold testing, Korean-language filing and the consistency of local facts with the global group narrative.

Operational CultureSouth Korean practice is filing- and documentation-driven, with detailed emphasis on CRIT, transaction data, functional analysis, comparables, Korean-language readiness and NTS audit support.
Legal Framework OrientationThe arm's length principle and associated transfer pricing procedures are codified in the Adjustment of International Taxes Act, its Enforcement Decree and Enforcement Rule, in alignment with OECD concepts.
Commercial ContextSouth Korea is a major centre for electronics, semiconductors, automotive, shipbuilding, technology, R&D, manufacturing, distribution and Asia-Pacific group operations.
Certainty FrameworkSouth Korea provides unilateral, bilateral and multilateral APAs, including statutory rollback possibilities, alongside tax treaty MAP and corresponding-adjustment mechanisms.

Process Flow

The process flow explains how South Korean transfer pricing work usually progresses from overseas related-party transaction mapping to CRIT, contemporaneous documentation and possible certainty or controversy management. It matters because Korea's framework combines dual thresholds, formal report filing and continuing documentation duties below those thresholds.

1. Overseas Related-Party Transaction MappingIdentify transactions with overseas related parties connected to South Korea, including goods, services, licensing, financing, R&D, manufacturing, distribution and permanent establishment dealings.
2. CRIT Threshold AssessmentAssess whether Korean gross sales exceed KRW 100 billion and aggregate overseas related-party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion, triggering master file and local file filing.
3. Functional AnalysisAnalyse what each party actually does, controls and assumes in operational and economic terms, including functions, assets, risks and intangible contributions.
4. Method Selection and Comparability ReviewChoose the most appropriate method and prepare comparable support, financial analysis and arm's length calculations consistent with Korean and OECD practice.
5. CRIT or Contemporaneous Documentation BuildPrepare master file and local file where thresholds are met, or maintain reasonable contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where the CRIT package is not required.
6. CbCR Notification and FilingAssess CbCR group status, file the notification within six months where required and submit the CbC report within the statutory 12-month period.
7. NTS Audit, APA or MAP RouteIf uncertainty or controversy arises, respond to NTS information requests, pursue unilateral, bilateral or multilateral APA, or use treaty MAP as relevant.
Typical OutputsCRIT, master file, local file, CbCR, contemporaneous documentation, functional analysis, benchmarking, intercompany agreements, Korean-language support, audit response papers and APA documentation.
Decision Tree

The decision tree simplifies threshold questions that commonly determine the right South Korean transfer pricing approach.

  1. Identify whether the transaction is with an overseas related party and has South Korean tax relevance.
  2. Confirm the actual functions performed, assets used, economically significant risks controlled and intangible contributions of each party.
  3. Assess whether annual Korean gross sales exceed KRW 100 billion and aggregate overseas related-party transactions exceed KRW 50 billion.
  4. Determine whether the group meets the KRW 1 trillion CbCR threshold and whether a six-month notification or 12-month report filing is required.
  5. Choose the most appropriate method and prepare Korean-language CRIT or reasonable contemporaneous documentation with comparable and financial support.
  6. Decide whether ordinary documentation is sufficient or whether unilateral, bilateral or multilateral APA, rollback or MAP planning should be considered.

Operating Constraints & Risks

Operating constraints identify the recurring friction points that affect transfer pricing execution in South Korea.

Dual-Threshold RiskIncorrect measurement of Korean gross sales or aggregate overseas related-party transactions can result in missed master-file and local-file CRIT filing obligations.
CbCR Timing RiskCbCR notification follows a six-month deadline while the CbC report follows a 12-month deadline, creating separate compliance controls for qualifying groups.
Korean-Language RiskLocal file and supporting materials must meet Korean-language requirements, requiring early localisation of group documentation and financial data.
Below-Threshold Documentation RiskTaxpayers below CRIT thresholds may incorrectly assume there is no documentation obligation, despite the requirement to maintain reasonable contemporaneous support for overseas related-party transactions.
Penalty ExposureFailure to submit a required local file or master file can result in penalties of KRW 30 million per missing file, creating potential combined exposure of KRW 60 million where both files are not filed.
